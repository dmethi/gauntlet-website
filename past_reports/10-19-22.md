
In what was yet another sluggish week in NFL scoring this week (scoring is down like 20% compared to last year), I also woke up feeling quite sluggish Tuesday morning.

So apologies for the late note, but I’m going to keep this one short and will conserve my energy for what will be a meaty midseason review that will come out at exactly the midway point in the fantasy regular season. For now, let’s just dive into the action.

Madurais (76.72) [4-2] vs Aman (79.36) [5-1]
In a matchup that was labeled to be an absolute heater between two of the three 4-1 teams in the league, this ended up being one of the lowest scoring matchups of the year. That’s not to say it wasn’t entertaining — this matchup ended up coming down to a long field goal at the end of SNF that the Cowboys kicker just missed, preventing the Madurais from securing the points they needed to edge ahead of Aman and secure the win. In retrospect though, the fate of this matchup may have been determined with the way the Bills / Chiefs game ended. Aman’s QB Josh Allen balled out, and Mahomes played well but completely sold on a potential game-winning drive at the end of the game. Had he gone for 20 yards passing instead of throwing a pick on that last drive, the Madurais would’ve secured the dub. Ouch.

But my god was this matchup loaded with busts; there were enough eggs laid in this matchup to provide brunch for the whole squad. There were EIGHT players combined between the two teams that scored below 5 points, and another three that failed to clear 7.

GIF by Paul Robertson
For the Madurais, a late Dalvin Cook 50 yard touchdown saved what was shaping up to be a humiliating performandce, headlined by Lockett and Dobbins only combining for 4 points after combining for 30 points just a week ago. Nothing else much to note besides the fact that this was an unusual bust performance - this is only their second time failing to hit 115 points this year.

For Aman, he disgustingly got bailed out by two Eagles players (Miles Sanders and AJ Brown) who combined for 30 points on Sunday night to barely give him the win. Those two, along with Josh Allen, combined for more than 75% of Aman’s points for the week in what ended up being a shockingly top-heavy performance. It was enough to get the job done this week, and Aman now moves to 5-1, still yet to score between 95 and 125 points on the year.

Sitarams (104.40) [1-5] vs Akshay (120.90) [3-3]
Heartbreaker for the Sitarams, who slide to 1-5, still awaiting their first performance over 110 points on the year. Oddly enough, this was a week where a score of 104 points was not entirely shameful, as it was enough to beat 7 other teams this week. But what’s the saying? “Fortune doesn’t favor the weak” or something like that, which certainly proved true this week.

On the one week where they finally saw Kittle catch a ball and saw Burrow play like a real QB, they also saw Aaron Jones pull a Mixon (9 carries 19 yards), Clyde pull a, well Clyde (9 carries 33 yards), and Hollywood Brown collect an injury that will keep him sidelined till the fantasy playoffs at minimum. It’s pretty embarrassing that their waiver wire addition Ken Walker instantly slots in as the best player on their team, and to add insult to injury, Mixon put up an efficient 17 fantasy points on 12 touches this week, putting up almost as much as Jones, Clyde, and Hollywood combined.

Sad Frustration GIF by GEICO
Far and away the favorites to land a bye in the toilet bowl this year, the Sitarams don’t have much more to play for than pride at this point, already 4 games back in the divisional race.

Different story for Akshay, who rebounded nicely after 2 straight Ls, putting up the 3rd highest total of the week and his own personal high for the season despite Cooper Kupp putting up his personal low for the year. After routinely pulling the wrong strings with his start / sit decisions all year, you’ve gotta give the main some credit for nailing it with his decisions to start Trevor Lawrence and Gabe Davis, who combined for 40 points, and a recap of Akshay’s week would be incomplete without mentioning Breece Hall who looks like an absolute beast, playing a huge role in embarrassing the Packers at home. Hell yeah.

With the win, Akshay regains some momentum and gets back to .500 on the year.

Varun (80.92) [2-4] vs 👑 Anish (128.42) [3-3]
In a battle between the SF IB roommates, Anish absolutely unloaded on Varun, releasing all the pent up anger from his interactions with his manager as well as being a perennial cellar dweller since 2019 to power himself to put up a score that was not only the highest score of the week, but his personal best performance since his finals performance in 2019. Live look at Anish on Sunday night:

Fuck Yeah Reaction GIF
Anish’s superstar trio of Chase, Tyreek, and Kamara led the way in this one, combining for nearly 70 points, almost as much as Varun’s entire team. This is what Anish had in mind when he paid the big bucks for these guys at the draft. It took 6 weeks for them to finally look the part, but if this is a sign of things to come, Anish’s team might actually be back.

Different story for Varun, whose team is firmly in a downward spiral. A roster highlighted by Russel Wilson and Melvin Gordon already faced a massive uphill battle to win, but Varun insisted on making it even harder on himself by self-sabotaging and starting an inactive Michael Thomas. He’ll need a lot more to go his way than the return of Davante Adams to his lineup if he wants to avoid securing an early ticket to the toilet bowl, but it’s hard to see where his team goes from here.

Shivang (106.90) [5-1] vs 🤡 Kishan (63.32) [2-4]
In another matchup pitting two early season standouts against each other, Kishan’s team choked in an epic fashion, putting up the lowest total of any team yet this season letting Shivang coast to an easy win. This is now the 5th straight week that Shivang has faced an opponent in the bottom-half of weekly league scoring, and he’s yet to face a team that has cleared 110 points since Week 1. That’s not to say he hasn’t earned his 5-1 record; it’s just that the only time Shivang has faced a strong opponent on the year, he’s lost. Call Shivang the 2022 Minnesota Vikings (who somehow are getting ranked at #4 in most power rankings behind only the Eagles, Bills, and Chiefs…….mad sus). Like with the Minnesota Vikings, only time will tell what this team’s ceiling truly is.

All he needed this week was contributions from his stars - Lamar, Ekeler, Pittman, and Kelce combined for nearly 75 points, and despite receiving next to nothing from the rest of his roster, it was more than enough to power him to a win. He’s managed to stay relatively clean from a health standpoint, but it’s very fair to ask questions about the depth that Shivang has behind his top options who have routinely contributed nothing to his team. Either way, Shivang is one of two teams at 5-1 on the year, and is firmly in the driver’s seat in his divisional race.

Kishan on the other hand…what happened my guy? The loss of Derrick Henry (on bye) completely devastated his team. Typical reliable producers Kareem Hunt, Nick Chubb, and Dallas Goedert combined for only 13 points, and Brady choked in a losing effort to the fucking Steelers. There’s not much to say but bleh. I think it’s more or less fair to characterize this week as an outlier performance for the man, but his margin for error is slowly slipping away. He now falls to 2-4, somehow with a worse record than Anish and I despite pretty comfortably holding the edge in points scored. He’ll need to translate those points into wins if he wishes to make the playoffs in what is proving to be an extremely tight race this year.

Ishan (124.48) [3-3] vs Jeffrey (69.72) [2-4]
In what has now become a routine occurrence, Jeffrey’s team got absolutely whipped into submission by Ishan, losing by over 50 points. Jeffrey now is been on the losing end of 2 of the 5 matchups that have been decided by 50 points or more. Maybe he likes the punishment, idk. Regardless, in a battle of 2-3 teams fighting to stay above the bottom tier of teams, it was Ishan who ended up coming out comfortably on top.

The story of this one is all about roster management. Ishan, being opportunistic this week, received 36 points from waiver wire studs Geno Smith and Deon Jackson. Jeffrey received 8 points from his $36 QB Herbert, and for some reason benched his elite WR Waddle in favor of Curtis fucking Samuel. That’s borderline criminal level of roster neglect.

Ishan received additional help from Stefon Diggs and Mark Andrews, and now has cleared 115 points in 4 of 6 weeks on the year. He now likely gets back Dak Prescott and D’Andre Swift, so Ishan’s team looks to be as strongly positioned as any team to make a serious push for the playoffs.

Jeffrey, on the other hand, is spiraling. He potentially has both a QB crisis and RB crisis on his hands, with Conner facing health issues and Herbert / Montgomery facing uncertain week-to-week roles. Outside of Fournette and probably Waddle, it’s hard to see where reliable production is going to come from on his team, and considering that he’s now 2-4, he’s going to have to figure that out fast, or else his dreams of repeating his miracle playoff run of last year are going to be quickly extinguished.

Methis (96.32) [3-3] vs Anant (94.30) [3-3]
This was an absolute bailout. Somehow, Krish and I did not get punished for starting the Cowboys defense against Anant’s Jalen Hurts in Philadelphia on Sunday Night — we were the beneficiaries of Hurts failing to hit 26 points for only the 2nd time this year, and the Cowboys late offensive surge against the Eagles defense did just enough to get my team a 2 point win, keeping us only 1 game back of the divisional lead despite failing to clear 100 points for what is now the 5th straight week. Bailout.

How did we crawl our way to 96 points? Mostly on the backs of Mondre and Saquon, who combined for 41 points. Saquon could’ve made this win a little more comfortable by going into the fucking end zone on his last carry of the day instead of sitting down at the one, but can’t fault our guy for being a consummate professional and a true team player. That’s what we’re looking for from the guys in our locker room, and you know what they say, “All’s well that ends well”. Daniel Jones and Justin Jefferson did just enough with reliable performances of their own to get our team across the finish line this week, and now Krish and I get to eagerly look ahead to the return of Sun God, Tua, and Olave to our lineup. Full steam ahead baby.

Meanwhile for Anant, he once again struggled to find production outside of Hurts and McCaffrey. Ertz, Etienne, and his Kicker each chipped in roughly 10 points each, but outside of that, Anant collected mostly scraps from everyone else. Still searching for a true complete team performance, Anant picked up his second L in a row falling back to .500, and he will hope to get back in the win column next week.
