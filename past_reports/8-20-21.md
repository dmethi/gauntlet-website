
I’m sure we’re all nose deep in our spreadsheets preparing for the draft, but I wanted to take a moment to reflect on an eventful season last year. I spoke with every manager in the league to gain some insight about everyone’s mentality during some of the highs and lows of last year as well as heading into this year.

Let’s get to it.

1st place: Anant
Our defending champion started off last season with a bang, going 7-2 in the first 9 games before losing 3 straight putting his playoff hopes in jeopardy. His response? Winning the next 4 games and the league. That’s championship material.

Our reigning champ, you had a blazing stretch in the playoffs led by stellar waiver wire picks, overcoming the loss of your $70+ man Christian McCaffrey. What went right last season, and how do you plan to build upon that finish in your quest to repeat?

At the end of the day, as management, we had faith in our abilities to put together a winning roster. By the final few weeks of the season, name value meant nothing to us. We were committed to putting together a starting lineup of individuals who believed in our message, and who believed we could overcome adversity and still win it all.

You had a novel approach to team management last year, often benching underperforming players to send a message in the midst of a 4-game losing streak that nearly saw you play yourself out of the playoffs. Clearly can’t argue with your results, but could you describe what you communicated to your team during this trying phase of the season, and will you be employing similar team management strategies this year to get the best out of your players?

Obviously, it’s never ideal when you lose a player who you spent 36% of your budget on. but in terms of managerial strategy, I like to employ a no bullshit attitude. We all knew losing a player we invested 36% of our draft capital in would really hurt, but some players on the team seemed ready to call it a bit too early. As a result we had to end up benching some quality players and holding them out of practice. Just because the team seemingly wasn’t as deep some players got too comfortable and thought their spot on the roster was guaranteed. But as a former championship winning team, we understood what it takes to win, and we had no tolerance for complacency. I don’t want to give too many details about what was said in the locker room. but in the midst of a 4 game skid, for a team with championship aspirations, I’m sure you can imagine it wasn’t all roses and dandelions.

Tell the league what to expect from you this year - why are you confident that this is your year?

Going into this year, we are even more committed to a no bullshit attitude. we understand there’s a lot of competition this year, but after winning the championship without the most expensive player in the league, there’s no reason to think that we can’t do it again.

Confident words from Mr. Jindani, who granted us an insightful window into his mindset during a gritty championship run. I’m sure all of us (some more than others) have a thing or two to learn from his managerial approach.

Luck definitely played a role into our playoff run, and we are not going to question it. Let the luck keep coming our way.
