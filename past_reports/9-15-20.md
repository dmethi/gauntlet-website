Hunter (1-0) vs Methis (0-1)
I don’t normally get this salty about fantasy football, but after all the excitement of the start of the season, I was seriously ticked off with all the things that went wrong. Let’s list them out:

Miles Sanders, my RB1, was ruled out on Saturday.

I started the top 2 Colts RBs, but they both got vultured by fucking Nyheim Hines. TWICE. Oh, and Marlon Mack tore his achilles midway through the second quarter.

Kyler Murray threw a 33 yard touchdown to DeAndre Hopkins. This touchdown was overturned, and on the next play, Kenyan Drake, Hunter’s RB, pounded it in for the score. That’s a 10 point swing.

James Conner, my RB2, injured his ankle early in the second quarter of his game.

Daniel Jones, Hunter’s starting QB, threw a garbage time TD with 1:30 left in the game. I had the Steelers defense. That’s a 6 point swing.

eric cartman bed GIF by South Park 
Despite it all, I still had a chance going into the final game of this week, holding on to a 2.5 point lead over Hunter with only Jonnu Smith left on his team. Smith got a 25 yard catch on a screen just a couple minutes into the game which put me out of my misery. Sigh.

In any case, Calvin Ridley (SMD Hunter) and Kyler Murray were bright spots in an otherwise bleak week for me, as the two of them accounted for over 50% of my points. If this week is any indication, I’ll need a lot of weeks like this from them if my team has any shot at contending.

Meanwhile, Hunter was able to overcome a relatively quiet week from his WRs by receiving almost 60 points from his RB trio of Kamara, Drake, and David Johnson who looks to be rejuvenated after looking like a bloated Eddie Lacy last year. With some better weeks from his WRs, Hunter has a great shot of breaking out of the middle of the pack and being a top contender for the ship.

Anant (1-0) vs Jeffrey (0-1)
Anant must’ve been seriously pissed off after the 9th place ranking he received in the post-draft analysis. His team had an absolutely juggernaut weak, ranking in the top-5 in the league in scoring from every position besides his defense, which happened to be the only one of his players who scored below 10 points. While Noah Fant and Cam Newton exceeded expectations, no one else on his roster really had a “blow up” week (27 points from McCaffrey is pretty standard), which speaks to the strength of his roster from top to bottom.

Jeffrey’s week started with a bang as he got to watch his rookie stud RB Clyde carve up the Texans on Thursday Night Football. In the end, he struggled with some of his studs playing like “smelly shite” as Gallup, Waller, Moore, and Mixon all underperformed. He’s got plenty of help on the bench, as he’s got Malcom Brown, Parris Campbell, and James Robinson stashed. Somehow he picked them all up before Week 1, and those 3 are among the most popular waiver wire picks this week. Unbelievable.

Ishan (1-0) vs Sitarams (0-1)
Boy am I relieved I was wrong. I can finally wash the poor taste out of my mouth from praising the Sitarams’ team last week so much as they simply got dealt a plain and simple ass-whooping from Ishan.

Dominant performances from Josh Jacobs and Chris Carson put this game quickly out of reach from the get go on Sunday, and the Sitarams just didn’t have an answer to the KO that they delivered. The Sitarams are also one of my prime rebound candidates as Saquads will surely average more than 1 YPC next game and Mahomes will certainly throw for more than 200 yards next game as well, creating more opportunity for Tyreek to eat.

Kishan (1-0) vs Akshay (0-1)
Despite Kishan’s $20 man Drew Brees putting up disappointing numbers (worst QB starter in the league), Kishan was able to quite easily dispatch Akshay en route to an opening week victory. He was powered by exceptional performances by Dalvin, Julio, and Hopkins as those three combined to provide almost 65% of his output for the week even though neither Hopkins nor Julio scored a touchdown. By the looks of it, he’s got as dominant a player trio as there is in the league, and he’ll be a tough one to beat week in and week out.

We have a quick verdict on Akshay’s strategy of prioritizing the veterans: absolute and complete failure. Rob Gronkwoski, Tom Brady, Mark Ingram, and Keenan Allen all looked underwhelming, and it certainly doesn’t help that his top player Michael Thomas is looking at a multi-week absence with a high ankle sprain. With bottom-4 performances at every one of his positions besides kicker, the present and future both look bleak for Akshay, and barring a miraculous turnaround, his team looks like its destined for another last-place finish. No way he flakes out of a punishment again.

The analytics weren’t wrong about Aman’s team - his team is loaded, and potentially for the first time in his fantasy football career, he looks ready to compete. This man is making trade offers, changing his team name, and even making a (albeit late) move to replace an injured starter with an upside WR on Monday night. This is the most engaged I’ve ever seen Aman, and that might be the biggest surprise of all from Week 1. Let’s hope it stays that way.

proud GIF
In any case, Aman benefited from the 2nd-half offensive explosion in the Vikings-Packers game as he had both Davante Adams and Adam Thielen, notching far and away the best WR performance in the league. While he should be assessed a penalty for starting a Packer against the Vikings, his team still would’ve won with strong supporting performances from Lamar Jackson and the Saints Defense as well. Aman has started off the season strong and currently sits at the top of the league.

Unfortunately for Varun, his team had essentially lost by the time the 3pm games rolled around on Sunday because of Thielen and Adams. He still had a strong week led by solid performances from Patrick Mahomes, Ezekiel Elliott, and Raheem Mostert, but his high investment in the Browns, specifically OBJ and Chubb, backfired spectacularly as the two combined for about as many points as Aman’s Ronald Jones. That is not a recipe for success, and if Varun wants any chance of competing, he’ll need bounce back weeks from both.

Here are a few of my takeaways from Week 1:

No real bad beats this week, which is a surprise! There’s no one that scored more than the league average this week and lost, and the only player who won after scoring in the bottom half of points scored for the week was Kishan, and he only scored about 10 points below league average.

Most fantasy football analysts were recommending people invest in RBs early and heavily given the depth at receiver. Shivang and Aman completely bucked that trend, and despite having two of the worst RB performances of the week, are sitting at 4th and 1st in the power rankings, respectively.

Currently, the brother pairs of the Methis, the Sitarams, and the Madurais are sitting at 9th, 10th, and 11th. @Aditya @Akhil I think our little brothers are bad karma, time to cut the dead weight? Just kidding.

In the current top-6, we only have 1 person with a history of past fantasy football success (in this league) - Anant. The other 5 spots are taken by the the 2 newcomers (Hunter and Ishan) along with 3 players who’ve generally struggled to make the playoffs (Kishan, Shivang, and Aman). Will one of these new faces at the top break through this season? The year is 2020, so anything can happen.

Some things, however, will never change. Akshay’s redemption campaign from last year’s last place finish is off to a dreadful start. Let’s not forget that this man still has yet to take the ACT hungover from last year’s punishment. Any proposals to escalate it if he finishes in last yet again?
